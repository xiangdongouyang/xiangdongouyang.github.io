Protecting Your Design When You Send Drawings to a Chinese Supplier

2026-10-30 · 5 min read · X.d Prototype Engineering Team

Sending a new EV housing to an overseas supplier is an act of trust. Buyers in the USA and Germany ask us the same question in almost every first email: how do I know my design stays mine? Here is a practical, non-legal answer from the manufacturing side — what actually protects you, and what is theater.

1. Sign the NDA before the STEP file

A mutual NDA costs nothing and takes minutes. Send it before the 3D model, not after. Any supplier that resists signing one for a legitimate prototype project is telling you something useful. We sign customer NDAs routinely, and we can also provide our own template if yours is not ready.

2. Sequence what you share

You rarely need to send everything at once:

  • Step 1 — quote: 2D PDF with critical dimensions, no 3D model. Enough to price machining time and material.
  • Step 2 — DFM: STEP file, still without the assembly context or the full product architecture.
  • Step 3 — production: full data package, including material certs and test specs.

Each step is a checkpoint: you learn how the supplier behaves before you hand over the family jewels.

3. Watermark and flatten

PDFs with a large “CONFIDENTIAL — [company] — not for distribution” watermark and no embedded 3D are harder to reuse casually. Flatten layers, remove metadata, and avoid sending native CAD (SolidWorks/Creo) unless the supplier must edit the model.

4. What an NDA can and cannot do

An NDA creates a contractual claim if your data is misused. It does not physically stop a file from being copied, and enforcing it across borders is expensive. So treat the NDA as one layer, not the whole defence:

  • Contract — NDA + written confidentiality clause in the purchase order.
  • Access — one named engineer handles your file, not a shared folder for the whole shop.
  • Data — files stored on access-controlled drives, deleted on request after the project.
  • Behaviour — a supplier that shows other customers’ drawings in the first meeting will show yours too.

5. Judge the supplier, not just the paperwork

The strongest signal is not the NDA template — it is whether the supplier already treats confidentiality as normal. Signs: they ask which features are critical instead of asking for the whole assembly, they do not forward your drawings to a third-party factory without telling you, they can explain which operations are done in-house and which are subcontracted.

6. Our policy, stated plainly

Drawings and 3D models sent to X.d Prototype are treated as confidential, shared only with the staff working on your project, and deleted on request after completion. We do not publish customer parts, dimensions or images without written permission, and we do not resell customer designs. NDAs are welcome before any technical exchange.

Checklist before you hit send

  1. NDA signed and filed.
  2. 2D PDF first, STEP second.
  3. Watermark on all PDFs; metadata stripped.
  4. Written confirmation of who has access.
  5. A deletion clause in the PO or a separate email.

If you would like to start with a document exchange under NDA, contact us — we can review drawings and return a DFM report without your data ever leaving the project team.
